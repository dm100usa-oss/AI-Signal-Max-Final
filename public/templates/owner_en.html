How websites get into AI recommendations

Today's users prefer to get ready-made answers rather than a list of links. AI assistants have adapted to this: they generate direct answers by selecting the most relevant sources. To appear in these answers more often, a website must meet three requirements:

1 Be technically accessible to AI
2 Have clear, direct content — provide precise answers in a specific category
3 Be trusted by AI as a reliable and safe source of information

1. Technical accessibility

AI platforms scan websites automatically. For your site to be accessible for analysis, it must be open to bots, indexed in Google and Bing, load quickly (under 2–3 seconds), work correctly on mobile devices, have clean URLs, and not contain duplicate pages.

2. Clear content and ready-made answers

This is an important distinction: when responding to a query, AI doesn't look for a website — it looks for an answer. It first selects pages with the right answer, then evaluates the site itself and includes the link. Each page should directly answer a specific user question, not just describe a service or company.

For example, for a pipe repair company:

✗ Our company understands even the subtleties of pipe repair, not just common situations
✓ Our company repairs water pipes
✓ How much does pipe repair cost in the city
✓ How to quickly fix a pipe leak
✓ When to replace pipes in your home

AI usually doesn't rewrite complex text — it takes already clear, concise pieces and uses them in answers. Page text must contain simple wording, short paragraphs, and clear sections. It's also important that the site covers one topic and is perceived by AI as a topical authority in its category.

Key principle: content should be written so it can be taken and inserted directly as a ready answer to a user.

3. Trust as a reliable source

AI selects websites that look trustworthy and back it up with facts. Your site must include: information about the company and contact details, clear authorship or expertise, real reviews and examples of work, specific results and numbers.

Consistent information across all pages is also important — if data contradicts itself, AI reduces trust in the site. Additional trust signals include off-site mentions: a Google profile, company directories, links in articles. AI looks not only at your site but also at whether you exist on the internet at all.

Key principle: AI selects not just information, but a reliable source. It must be clear, verifiable, and confirmed.
